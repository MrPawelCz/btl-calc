📐 SDLT bands & breakdown
Standard UK 2025/26 bands (England & NI, HMRC; from 1 April 2025):
£0-£125,000: 0% · £125k-£250k: 2% · £250k-£925k: 5% · £925k-£1.5M: 10% · £1.5M+: 12%
Additional property surcharge (HRAD): +5% on entire price (above £40k de-minimis)
Non-UK resident surcharge: +2% on entire price (day-count test, NOT citizenship)
First-time buyer relief: 0% up to £300k, 5% on £300k-£625k (no relief above £625k)
Surcharges stack: e.g. non-resident BTL = standard + 5% HRAD + 2% non-res.
Ltd company / SPV: calculated as 'Additional property' (standard + HRAD). The 15% flat-rate regime applies only to non-natural-person purchases of single dwellings >£500k where the property is NOT used in a property business; for BTL it's almost never optimal.

📐 Formulas and assumptions (notes)
# Stamp Duty (SDLT) - England & NI 2025/26 Standard bands (slice-by-slice): £0 - £125,000 -> 0% £125k - £250k -> 2% £250k - £925k -> 5% £925k - £1,500,000 -> 10% > £1,500,000 -> 12% Surcharges (applied on the WHOLE price, not slices): + HRAD (additional property / BTL / 2nd home / Ltd company): +5%, above £40k de-minimis + Non-UK resident (< 183 days in UK in 12 months before completion): +2% Surcharges STACK. E.g. non-resident BTL = standard + 5% HRAD + 2% non-res. # Non-UK resident test for SDLT (IMPORTANT) This is a DAY-COUNT test, not a nationality test. HMRC ignores: - citizenship (you can be Polish/EU and still be UK-resident for SDLT) - immigration status / visa type - the UK Statutory Residence Test (which is about Income Tax) Individual: UK-resident for SDLT if present in UK >= 183 days in the 12 months before the effective date of the transaction. Company: UK-resident for SDLT if UK-resident for Corporation Tax. BUT: a UK-incorporated 'close company' (typical SPV with <= 5 controlling shareholders) is treated as non-resident if any controller fails the 183-day test. So a Polish-owned Ltd is non-resident for SDLT UNLESS all controllers themselves pass the 183-day test. If bought jointly with spouse/civil partner: if one is UK-resident, both are treated as UK-resident. Refund: if you become a UK resident within 12 months of purchase, you can claim a refund of the 2% surcharge. First-time buyer relief (price <= £625k only): £0 - £300k -> 0% £300k - £625k -> 5% Above £625k -> relief lost, falls back to standard Note: FTB relief NEVER stacks with HRAD (you can't be FTB and own another property). # Stage 1: Purchase Total Investment Cost = Price + Refurb + StampDuty + Legal + Sourcing + PM Fee + Structural Survey + Other custom costs + Final BTL setup (arrangement/booking/valuation/broker/legal) + if BRRR + 1st BTL on: 1st BTL setup costs + refurb-period interest Note: if ARRANGEMENT FEE is "added to loan": - it's NOT in Total Investment Cost (not paid out of pocket) - it increases the loan amount (and the interest base) - effectively repaid over the life of the mortgage in the monthly payment Mortgage (loan amount) = LTV% × base (DUV or Price) + optionally capitalised arrangement fee Cash needed (deposit) = Total Investment Cost - Mortgage # Stage 2: Monthly cashflow Mortgage interest-only: Mortgage × rate% / 12 Monthly costs = MortgagePay + Mgmt%×Rent + Insurance + Maint%×Rent + Voids%×Rent + CompanyCosts + Other Cashflow = Rent - Monthly costs # KPI - Yields Gross Yield (DUV) = (Rent × 12) / DUV - UK industry standard, gross rent / value Gross Yield (Price) = (Rent × 12) / Price - same vs. purchase price (BRRR shows how cheaply you bought yield) Net Yield (DUV) = (Rent - operating costs WITHOUT mortgage) × 12 / DUV Net Yield (Price) = (Rent - operating costs WITHOUT mortgage) × 12 / Price -> Net yield is what you'd earn if buying cash (no mortgage). Mortgage is a financing cost, not a property cost - that's why it only kicks in cashflow/ROE. # ROE ROE = (Annual cashflow / Cash needed) × 100% -> annual cash returned as % of own money invested. -> mortgage leverage pushes ROE above yield - the whole point of BTL on credit. # UK interest rate (notes) - FIXED RATE (most BTL): SONIA swap rate (2Y/5Y) + lender margin ≈ visible rate - TRACKER: BoE Base Rate + margin (changes when BoE moves) - SVR (Standard Variable Rate): expensive (~7-9%), kicks in after fixed period - ALWAYS refinance before. - Fixed period: 2/3/5/10 years. ERC for early exit typically 1-5% of balance. # Investment strategies (pick one) ## 💵 Cash buy Buy outright, no mortgage anywhere. Sections 2-4 are hidden. Cash needed = Total Investment Cost (the full purchase). MonthlyCosts = operating costs only (no mortgage payment). ROE here equals net yield - leverage doesn't apply. ## 🏦 Standard BTL (single mortgage at purchase) One mortgage event. The property is ready to let (minimal/no refurb). LTV is applied to PURCHASE PRICE. Mortgage = LTV% × Price + optional capitalised arrangement fee Cash needed = Total Investment Cost - LTV% × Price Refurb period and 1st BTL are not used. ## 🔄 BRRRR (Buy -> Refurb -> Rent -> Refinance -> Repeat), no bridging Two events. The remortgage event is what sections 2 & 3 (Mortgage + Mortgage Setup Costs) describe - that's the FINAL, long-term BTL used in Cashflow / ROE / Scenarios. LTV is applied to DUV (Done Up Value, after refurb). Default path (cash purchase + remortgage): 1. Buy in cash 2. Refurb (no rent during refurb_months) 3. Let the property 4. Remortgage onto a BTL valued from DUV - 6-month rule: most lenders won't refinance earlier than 6 months after purchase - Goal: 75% LTV * DUV "pulls back" the deposit (because DUV > price + refurb) With 1st BTL toggled on (two-mortgage path): 1. Buy with 1st BTL (LTV * Purchase Price), separate rate / fixed period 2. Refurb_months pass with no rent; 1st BTL interest accrues -> refurb interest = 1stLoan * 1stRate% / 12 * refurb_months (added to Total Investment Cost) 3. Let the property 4. Remortgage as above. Setup costs are paid AGAIN (full set in sections 2 & 3). Note: 1st BTL is settled at remortgage. Its rate / fixed period only matter for refurb interest. The FINAL BTL (sections 2 & 3) is the one driving long-term cashflow. # What I look for - Cashflow > 0 is the absolute minimum, realistically I want > £150 / month buffer. - ROE > 10% is OK, > 15% is a good investment. - In the scenarios table I check what happens if rates go up 2-3pp - is cashflow still positive. - Remember: after the fixed period ends I have to refinance = another ~£1,500-£4,000 of costs.
