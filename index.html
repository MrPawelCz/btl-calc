How to read the numbersScope & formulas

Cashflow is a steady-state estimate

Rent less operating costs, reserves and interest-only payments. Annual cashflow is a full letting year, not the first year from purchase. No income or corporation tax, principal repayments, selling costs or capital growth are modelled.

Funding is not the same as equity

Cash needed upfront includes entered cash costs. For BRRR, cash left in the deal deducts refinance proceeds after repaying the purchase loan. A negative figure means additional cash released; it does not mean the deal is risk-free. Fees added to a loan increase debt, not cash proceeds.

Return on the cash you leave invested

ROE (cash-on-cash) = annual cashflow ÷ cash invested. With zero or negative cash left, the percentage is not meaningful and is shown as N/A.

Reading a sourcing offer

Offers often list purchase price, refurbishment, stamp duty, legal fees, done-up value (DUV), final rent, total investment cost, yield and ROE. Enter sourcing and project (sometimes “property”) management fees under Purchase costs. Funding breakdown itemises your full entered cost; ROE here deducts running costs and interest. Ask the sourcer how their yield and ROE are calculated before comparing figures.

A refinance is an assumption, not an offer

The model uses your chosen LTV and property value. A lender may lend less because of rental-cover tests, valuation, property condition or ownership-period rules. Review realistic rent and valuation evidence, fees and a higher-rate scenario.
